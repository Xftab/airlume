## 16 October 2026
- **4.3 CAMS-vs-ground validation built; first look on 30 hours.** `airlume/validation.py` pairs every station-hour with CAMS for Lahore and reports mean absolute difference, bias (CAMS minus station) and correlation per station, by hour of day and for the city mean, with 95% intervals from a moving-block bootstrap over hours (stations share one CAMS value per hour, so resampling rows would overstate certainty). AQI is compared only on the portal's own scale and from PM on both sides; the stored CAMS US AQI is never used, so the W1 mismatch cannot return. Preliminary: CAMS PM2.5 about 20 ug/m3 low (CI -28 to -4), the gap almost all at night; PM10 not tracked at all (r -0.16, bias -78); per-station bias -52 to +18. Correlation is highest with CAMS shifted 1--2 hours later; our own timestamps were checked first and are correct, so it is recorded as a finding to retest rather than corrected. Labelled preliminary everywhere; final run about 29 October. 10 tests. Write-up: `docs/research/cams-validation.md`.
- **Forecast questions were being answered with the present --- fixed.** A question about the future ("what will the air be like tomorrow?", "kal hawa kaisi hogi?") was routed correctly to `view="forecast"` (100% on that benchmark class), but `answer()` never read the forecast: it fell through to the station path and replied with current readings. The router was right and the sentence answered a different question. `nl/answer.py` now answers from the stored relayed forecasts, per source and each from its own latest issue, with the mean, peak and lowest two-hour window, the source's measured error, a city-level caveat when a station is named, "none published" for cities without a forecast, and no advice wording; times that cross midnight say "tomorrow". 13 tests. While doing it: `latest_forecast` returns the single latest issue across sources, which silently drops whichever source issued earlier, so the answer layer queries per source instead. Also corrected the plan's 4.6 exit wording: the benchmark's `best_time` class is retrospective and was always answered correctly; the forward-looking class is `forecast`.
- **The EPA forecast reaches only 8 hours ahead in our data** because the HTML parser reads the 8 points the page renders, while the snapshot's `aqiForecastData` carries 23. Recorded as a concrete reason for the snapshot migration of the forecast reader deferred to 4.1.
- **W1 solved: the portal uses its own NEQS-anchored AQI table, not the US EPA one.** With 24 consecutive hours and 204 rows carrying the portal's own dominant pollutant, the cause of the +8 bias was localised and then recovered outright. The portal computes AQI from the latest hour as the maximum of per-pollutant sub-indices, on breakpoints that sit on Pakistan's NEQS limits --- an AQI of 100 is exactly the legal short-term limit for PM2.5 (35), PM10 (150), NO2 (80) and O3 (130 ug/m3) --- with gases banded in ug/m3 rather than converted to ppb. Fitted band by band with r^2 = 1.00, it reproduces all 304 stored station-hours: **99.7% exact, 100% within one point, dominant pollutant 100%**, against 6.2% exact for the US 2012 table used until today. Every one of the 13 portal readings pinned in the tests is reproduced exactly. Implemented as `pk_portal`, now the default in `aqi.py`; US tables kept for comparison. Plan 1.7, the W1 row, Appendix D and the proposal's E3 corrected. Write-up: `docs/research/w1-harmonisation.md`.
- **How it was found, in four steps worth reporting.** (1) The dominance test agreed 94%, and where both sides said PM2.5 our sub-index was high on essentially every row --- so the bias lived inside the PM2.5 calculation, not in which pollutant won. (2) The concentration implied by the portal's AQI, over the one it displayed, varied by band (0.80 to 1.00) --- the signature of different breakpoints, not a scale factor. (3) NowCast was tested and ruled out: it cut the bias but worsened the error. (4) Regressing concentration on AQI within each band recovered the edges directly.
- **Why the 13 Oct identification looked right and was not.** The four hand-checked readings were smog-morning values of 141--311 ug/m3, where the US and Pakistan tables converge. The tables part most at 35--70 ug/m3, Lahore's ordinary afternoon, where the US table tops its 101--150 band at 55.4 and the portal at 70: a reading of 60 is "Moderate" on the portal and "Unhealthy for sensitive groups" on the US scale. Recorded because it is the cleanest example in the project of a sample chosen for convenience hiding the answer.
- **The rejected ozone hypothesis is explained.** The two rows that suggested an 8-hour maximum were the portal banding O3 in ug/m3 on a 65/130 scale while we converted to ppb: Gulberg III 82.39 -> 64 and Wagha 91.97 -> 71, both exactly the portal's published values. There was never an averaging effect.
- **Averaging window settled on 24 consecutive hours:** latest hour, mean error 0.0 against 20.2 (8-hour) and 26.8 (24-hour), at all eleven stations.
- **Limits kept honest.** PM2.5 above 350 and PM10 above 550 are extrapolated, not observed; November's smog will test the top band. The NO2 101--150 band rests on one point. Above the last fitted gas band the sub-index is refused rather than extrapolated. SO2 and CO have never been dominant, so their bands are NEQS-anchored placeholders. `aqi_computed` in the archive switches tables on 16 Oct; earlier rows are not rewritten, since the column is derived and recomputable.

## 15 October 2026
- **4.67 million people in Lahore live more than 5 km from a monitor.** The population half of 2.6 and most of 4.5 are done (`docs/research/population.md`), using WorldPop 2025 at 1 km and an OpenStreetMap extract. 33% of the city's people are beyond 5 km and only 32% are within 3 km, against 44% and 23% measured by *area* --- people are better covered than the average square kilometre, because the eleven stations sit in the built-up city while the bounding box includes farmland. Both figures are kept and the report never says "coverage" without saying which it means.
- **Took the 1 km raster over the 100 m one, deliberately.** The 100 m product is 438 MB and was a five-hour download on the project's connection; the coverage grid is 1 km, so it would have been averaged up on arrival. Matching the analysis resolution was the right call rather than a compromise, and the download was abandoned part-way.
- **Both big files are cut down once into committed CSVs** (72 KB and 103 KB) by `scripts/extract_population.py` and `scripts/extract_landuse.py`, so nothing at runtime needs `rasterio`, `osmium`, `shapely` or GDAL, and the raw 157 MB and 5.5 MB downloads stay in git-ignored `data/raw/`.
- **Area-optimal and population-optimal station sites disagree, and that is a finding.** The area heuristic put the first new station at 31.56 N, 74.20 E in the thinly populated west; weighting by people moves it to 31.46 N, 74.37 E in the dense south-east. The earlier recommendation was not wrong, it was answering a different question. Both are kept in the app side by side.
- **The source-proximity trade-off is reported, not resolved.** Ranking purely by population brings 3.15 million people within 5 km (33% -> 11% underserved); blending in closeness to major roads and industrial land at weight 0.3 brings 2.35 million (33% -> 16%) but places every monitor within 200 m of a major road. Weighting towards sources costs about 800,000 people of coverage. Which is correct depends on whether the network measures exposure or emissions, so the plan now reports both and picks neither.
- **Gains fall away fast:** the first proposed site serves 1.1 million people, the fifth 278,000. Most of the benefit comes from two or three well-placed monitors.
- **ISPRS comparison done as a method comparison; a numeric overlap is not possible.** The cited 2022 ISPRS Lahore siting study publishes no coordinates --- its nine recommended sites appear only as markers on a map --- and digitising them would be accurate to no better than 1--2 km, which is too soft to report as an overlap. Method, criteria and count are compared instead. Two findings came out of it: their criteria (commercial hubs, industry, traffic, drainage) are **source-led** while ours are **exposure-led**, which makes their work the closer analogue of our combined variant than of the population-only one and is a reason to present both; and the three-station network that motivated their nine recommendations has since grown to the eleven we collect from, so the question has moved from "how many stations" to "are they in the right places" --- which is what the 4.67 million figure answers. Asking the authors for coordinates is the honest route to a numeric overlap.
- **Known gaps recorded rather than glossed:** multipolygon relations are not assembled, so only closed ways become exclusion polygons and the Ravi river is under-caught (ten cells excluded in total: seven airport, two military, one water); road and industrial distances are measured to the nearest node rather than the nearest point on the line; and the comparison against the 2022 ISPRS Lahore siting study still needs that paper's coordinates.
- **Phase 2 closed except population; 4.5 and 4.6 advanced.** 2.3 time animation (play/pause, 24 h / 7 d / 30 d windows, scrubber, frame counter) and 2.4 wind layer are done, the wind coming from the stations' own instruments rather than a 45 km model cell. 4.6 lowest-predicted-window is built and shown for both relayed forecasts. 4.5 now produces the ranked five sites the plan asks for. 146 tests.
- **Wind direction convention pinned by test.** The portal reports the direction wind blows *from*; an arrow reads as motion, so it is drawn towards `direction + 180` while the label keeps the "from" wording. Getting this backwards would be invisible on screen and wrong in every figure in the report, so it is asserted rather than assumed.
- **4.6 caught pointing at the past.** The portal's 24-hour forecast still contains the hour it was issued in, so the "lowest window in the next 24 hours" initially selected an hour that had already gone. Hours before the current one are now excluded; pinned by test. The window is also refused across a gap in the series, so a mean is never reported over hours that were not adjacent.
- **4.6 is city-level, against the plan's original wording.** The plan said "for the nearest station". Neither source forecasts a station: the portal publishes one forecast for Lahore, and CAMS resolves to about one 45 km cell over the city. Naming a neighbourhood would imply precision that does not exist, so the answer names Lahore and the plan has been corrected rather than the code bent to match it.
- **Coverage figures are area, not population --- said plainly everywhere.** 2.6 and 4.5 both ask for the share of *population* beyond 5 km, which needs a WorldPop or GHSL raster this environment cannot download. Every figure is therefore labelled an area share, and the five proposed sites are marked provisional because the population, land-use and proximity-to-sources criteria are all still unapplied. Recorded rather than quietly reported as "coverage", which would read as people.
- **Decision: the snapshot's extra columns join data of record.** `portal_station_id`, `portal_dominant`, `lat`, `lon`, `temperature`, `humidity`, `wind_speed`, `wind_direction` added to `archive.COLS["readings"]` and the DuckDB `readings` table. Taken once the ozone test (below) showed the missing `portal_dominant` was what blocked a clean W1 answer; it also supplies 2.4's wind layer from a request we already make. Three things make it safe on existing data: the columns are **appended, never inserted**, so pre-15-Oct CSVs keep their column order and read back blank in the new fields; `Store.__init__` runs `ALTER TABLE ... ADD COLUMN IF NOT EXISTS` for each, because `CREATE TABLE IF NOT EXISTS` leaves an existing cache alone and would otherwise fail on the laptop while passing on a fresh database --- the same asymmetry that broke it on 14 Oct when the gas columns went in; and the HTML-table fallback, which produces none of them, still inserts with nulls. All three pinned by tests. Nothing needs rebuilding by hand. **Deferred in the same decision:** moving the trend and forecast readers onto the snapshot, since the HTML parsers work and have fixtures, and the change touches the forecast scorecard (4.1) --- better done when 4.1 is next worked on.
- **An ozone hypothesis raised and knocked down the same hour.** The snapshot carries the portal's own `major_pollutant`, giving a per-row check on our sub-index logic. On the first live round 9 of 11 stations agreed; the two that did not both had the portal naming O3, and in both its ozone sub-index implied about 1.4 times the ozone the same page displayed --- the signature of a maximum 8-hour average, which the US EPA specifies for ozone and not for PM. Tested against all 100 stored rows before it went any further: **not supported.** The portal exceeds every sub-index we compute in only 3 of 100 rows, with implied multipliers of 1.49, 2.85 and 3.51 rather than a consistent 1.4; the dominant pattern remains that we read higher, in 81 of 100 rows. What survives is only a time-of-day shape --- mean (portal minus ours) is negative at every Pakistan hour except 13:00 and 14:00, when ozone peaks --- on ten stations across one day. Recorded as a knocked-down hypothesis rather than quietly dropped, because the two-row version looked convincing and would have reached the report. **Consequence: the clean test needs `portal_dominant` on every row, which the archive drops, so the pending schema decision now blocks W1 rather than merely improving it.**
- **The portal publishes far more than it renders --- found via its embedded Livewire snapshot** (`docs/research/portal-snapshot.md`). `GET /lahore` carries the page's component state as ~150 KB of JSON in a `wire:snapshot` attribute, holding **two** Lahore station arrays: one of 11 and one of 10. The HTML table is rendered from the 10, so **Gulberg III has live values in the portal's own data and never appears in its table** --- the reason `LHR-GB3` had zero rows in every collected hour, while the page's own heading says "Lahore (11 stations)". The collector now reads the snapshot (`parse_lahore_snapshot`), falling back to the table parser. Decision: do not hard-code the path to the array; walk the snapshot and take the largest station array, so a redesign degrades rather than breaks.
- **Station coordinates are published after all, and one of ours was 6 km wrong.** The snapshot carries official latitude and longitude per station. Nine of our eleven IQAir-derived coordinates were right to within 130 m; **Multan Road was 6.13 km out** and Kahna Nau 0.58 km (both were neighbourhood-centroid guesses), and Punjab University's "medium confidence, 4 km SW of main campus" doubt is resolved --- the portal confirms that point exactly. `data/registry/stations.csv` now holds the official values with `confidence: official` and the portal's own station ids; the app's "station positions approximate" captions are gone. Coverage (C3/C7) recomputed: mean 5.0 -> 4.93 km, 45% -> 44% beyond 5 km, 22% -> 23% within 3 km; the recommended new-station location is unchanged. The EPA email should still go, since a published attribute is not a licence, but the coordinate question is closed.
- **Unmatched station labels are now logged.** `parse_lahore_html` previously dropped an unrecognised station name with a bare `continue` --- a station could leave the dataset in total silence. It did not cause the Gulberg III loss, but nothing would have reported it if it had. Also fixed name matching so the portal's two spellings ("Gulberg III" in the table, "Gulberg III, Lahore" in the JSON) both resolve.
- **Phase 1.6 data-quality checks built** (`airlume/pipeline/quality.py`, `scripts/quality_report.py`, app tab, 17 tests): gaps, stuck sensors, impossible values, spikes, partial rounds, per-station uptime. Design decision: **nothing is corrected automatically**, because the archive is the data of record --- suspect values are flagged and left in place for a human. Thresholds are published in the report rather than buried in code, and uptime is measured over hours the collector actually ran, so the irregular pre-trigger collection is not charged to the stations.
- **A quality check that fired on its own sampling irregularity --- caught before it reached the report.** The first version differenced adjacent *rows* rather than adjacent *hours* and reported ten spikes and one stuck sensor; every flagged timestamp sat exactly on a pre-trigger collection gap, so a five-hour change was being read as a one-hour jump. All eleven vanished once both checks were restricted to pairs one hour apart. Pinned by `test_large_jump_across_a_collection_gap_is_not_a_spike`. Recorded because the false numbers looked plausible enough to survive a casual read.
- **Gulberg III has never reported.** `LHR-GB3` is in the registry but has produced zero rows in all 10 collected hours --- not an intermittent dropout, as the 13 Oct note ("absent in one snapshot") implied. Every "10 of 11 stations" figure is this one station. To resolve on the laptop: check the live page for a different spelling (parser alias) or mark the station inactive.
- **W1 tested against stored data for the first time; the 8-hour averaging guess was wrong.** The latest-hour values reproduce the portal's AQI to a mean 5.4 points over 90 station-rows; an 8-hour trailing mean gives 24.3 and is worse at every station. The 2012 PM2.5 table beats the 2024 one, confirming the 13 Oct identification. A **+4 point systematic bias remains unexplained** (we read higher in 83% of rows) after ruling out a publication lag, integer truncation and the gas sub-indices. Consequence: the plan's "within 0--3 points" claim, which came from four hand-checked readings, was corrected in the Phase 0 section, Appendix D and the W1 row; 1.7's exit criterion is now "window decided on 24+ consecutive hours and the bias explained or quoted as an interval", not "tests pass". The EPD order's 8-hour averaging applies to the printed bulletins, not the live table. Working: `docs/research/w1-harmonisation.md`.
- **`aqi.py` crashed on NaN --- fixed.** `sub_index` guarded `None` but not NaN, and NaN fails every comparison silently (`NaN < 0` is false), so a gap in a rolling mean reached `int()` and raised. This is why `w1_check.py` had never produced output. A shared `_missing()` guard now covers both entry points; regression test added. 85 tests pass.
- **City-level archive rows are revised, not appended --- and two portal widgets are being conflated.** Lahore's 15 Oct 06:00 UTC city AQI was committed as 73 and later as 77. Measured across the eleven commits that touch the file: 4 of 10 revisions change 1--7 rows. Two distinct causes, neither of them what was first assumed (nothing in the pipeline computes a city value from our station rows; `n_stations` is always empty). **(a)** The home-page district ranking stamps its row with the page's "last updated" time rounded to the hour, so two collections inside one clock hour write the same key and the portal's own small drift (+/-1--2 points) silently overwrites. **(b)** The "AQI Trends (last 7 days)" widget and the home-page ranking publish *different* values for the same city-hour (73 against 77) but are archived under one source name, `punjab_epa`, so the archive key `(city_id, ts, source)` cannot hold both and the last writer wins. They are different series --- the trend is the portal's retrospective value, the ranking a live snapshot --- and conflating them loses the disagreement. Recommendation recorded for 1.6: give the trend widget its own source name, as the forecast tables already do. Not done unilaterally because it changes the semantics of data of record.
